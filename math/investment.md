### Gold to Silver Ratio

This ratio is used by precious metals investors as an indicator of the relative value of the two metals, helping to inform their trading strategies.

- High ratio: A high ratio suggests that silver is undervalued relative to gold. For example, if the ratio is 80:1, meaning one ounce of gold costs 80 ounces of silver, it could be a sign that silver is "cheap" and presents a potential buying opportunity for investors who believe the ratio will revert to its historical average.
- Low ratio: Conversely, a low ratio indicates that silver is expensive relative to gold. This might signal a buying opportunity for gold.
- Safe-haven indicator: The ratio can also serve as a measure of market sentiment. During times of economic uncertainty, investors often flock to gold as a traditional safe-haven asset, causing its price to rise faster than silver's and widening the ratio. Silver's value is more tied to industrial demand, so during periods of economic stability, silver can outperform gold, narrowing the ratio.

#### What's the neural range of gold to silver ratio?

- Over the last several decades, a "normal" or "neutral" range for the gold to silver ratio has been considered to be roughly <b>50:1 to 80:1.</b> 
- High ratio (above 80:1): Some investors may see this as a sign that silver is undervalued relative to gold and may consider buying silver or trading some gold for silver.
- Low ratio (below 50:1): Conversely, a low ratio could indicate that gold is undervalued relative to silver, potentially signaling a buying opportunity for gold.

#### Historical context

- In the 20th century, the average ratio was about <b>47:1</b>.
- Over the past 30 years, the average has been closer to <b>68:1</b>.
- The ratio has reached extreme levels in recent history, such as peaking above <b>125:1</b> during the market turmoil of March 2020 at the beginning of the COVID-19 pandemic.
